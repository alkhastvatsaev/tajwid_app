  1. Practice madd

    Written by Alkhast Vatsaev · Updated 2026-08-14

    Madd is lengthening a letter of madd (ا و ي) when the conditions are met. Natural madd (aṣlī, also called ṭabīʿī) is about two ḥarakāt. Connected or separate madd, madd lāzim, and others stretch longer — your muqriʾ decides the school and the count. Tilmidh shows where madd appears in the Uthmani tajweed text; the mic checks the word identity, not a stopwatch on six ḥarakāt.

    The durations at a glance

    The durations below follow riwāya Ḥafṣ ʿan ʿĀṣim by the Shāṭibiyya route, the most widespread. Other routes hold other lengths — one of several reasons a teacher remains necessary.

    Type of maddDuration
    ṭabīʿī (natural)2 ḥarakāt
    muttaṣil (hamza in the same word)4 to 5 ḥarakāt
    munfaṣil (hamza in the next word)4 to 5 ḥarakāt
    lāzim (permanent sukūn)6 ḥarakāt
    ʿāriḍ li-s-sukūn (stopping at a word end)2, 4 or 6 ḥarakāt

    The word beginners clip

    ٱلرَّحْمَٰنِ

    In ar-raḥmān you meet a madd shape that beginners often clip. In Ikhlāṣ, Allāh and aḥad teach you not to invent extra length where there is none, and not to starve length where the text marks it. Recite one word, wait until it is accepted, continue.

    The honest limit of the app

    No app replaces a teacher for madd timing. Tarteel and similar tools try cloud scoring; Tilmidh stays on-device Web Speech and word matching. Use us for fluency and order; use a muqriʾ for ijazah-level length.

    Frequently asked questions

    How long is a natural madd?

    Two ḥarakāt. It is the reference unit: every other duration is counted in multiples of it, up to six ḥarakāt for madd lāzim.

    What is the difference between muttaṣil and munfaṣil?

    Both last 4 to 5 ḥarakāt in Ḥafṣ, but muttaṣil has its hamza in the same word as the letter of madd, while munfaṣil has its hamza at the start of the next word.

    Does Tilmidh check the length of my madd?

    No. The mic checks that the word you said is the right one, not the exact length of the lengthening. Train the length by ear, then have your recitation confirmed by a muqriʾ — Tilmidh is a repetition tool, not a certification.
