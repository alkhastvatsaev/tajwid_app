  1. Recite Al-Kahf word by word

    Written by Alkhast Vatsaev · Updated 2026-08-15

    Al-Kahf is the eighteenth surah, one hundred and ten verses, revealed in Makkah. Many people read it on Friday. A hundred and ten verses is no longer a short surah you recite in one breath: it is endurance. If you speed up in the first story, you reach the later ones with a tired mouth and madd that quietly shrink.

    ٱلۡحَمۡدُ لِلَّهِ ٱلَّذِيٓ أَنزَلَ عَلَىٰ عَبۡدِهِ ٱلۡكِتَٰبَ وَلَمۡ يَجۡعَل لَّهُۥ عِوَجَاۜ

    Al-ḥamdu li-Llāhi lladhī anzala ʿalā ʿabdihi l-kitāba wa lam yajʿal lahu ʿiwajā. “Praise be to Allah, Who sent down the Book upon His servant and placed no crookedness in it.” Two traps already: the madd of alladhī (two ḥarakāt, not more), and the ʿayn in ʿabdihi / ʿiwajā, which many swallow or turn into a hamza.

    Four stories, four sessions

    The surah is not one block. It is four stories: the People of the Cave, the two gardens, Mūsā and al-Khiḍr, Dhū l-Qarnayn. Work one story per session. Always repeat the last two verses of the previous story to weld the join — that is where memory fails, not in the middle of a narrative.

    StoryVersesWhat you train
    People of the Cave9–26hamza of aḥsibta, ikhfāʾ, ghunnah of inna
    The two gardens32–44madd at verse ends, qalqalah on qāf
    Mūsā and al-Khiḍr60–82idghām, narrative pace, do not swallow names
    Dhū l-Qarnayn83–98qalqalah, emphatics, breath on the lists

    The verse that breaks first

    أَمۡ حَسِبۡتَ أَنَّ أَصۡحَٰبَ ٱلۡكَهۡفِ وَٱلرَّقِيمِ كَانُواْ مِنۡ ءَايَٰتِنَا عَجَبًا

    Verse 9, am ḥasibta anna aṣḥāba l-kahfi wa r-raqīmi kānū min āyātinā ʿajabā. Three things in one line. The ḥāʾ of ḥasibta is not an English h. The lām of the article merges into ar-raqīm: you read r-raqīm, not al-raqīm. And āyātinā carries a madd of two ḥarakāt, held, not approximated. Take this verse alone ten times before you continue the story.

    The rules that keep returning

    Madd first: Kahf’s verse endings are long, and Friday recitation is where people get caught — long at the start, short after verse 40. Then ghunnah on the many inna and anna. Then ikhfāʾ before sīn, shīn and kāf in the Cave narrative. Qalqalah returns on the qāf of kahf if you stop, and on dāl in waqf.

    Tilmidh loads Quran.com Uthmani tajweed and only advances when the mic has recognised the word. On a surah this long, the tool does not measure your six ḥarakāt: it stops you gliding. That is already a lot. Length and emphatics still need a muqriʾ.

    Open the mic on Al-Kahf

    If Kahf is too long today, go back to Al-Mulk (thirty verses, same endurance logic) or Ayat al-Kursi (one long verse, for breath). On Friday, one story held well beats a hundred and ten verses swallowed.

    Frequently asked questions

    Must I recite all of Al-Kahf on Friday?

    Many people do. For practice, no: one story per session, on the same weekday, builds a stable surah. Swallowing 110 verses at speed only trains you to shorten madd.

    Which story should I start with?

    The People of the Cave (verses 9–26). It is the best known, and verse 9 already concentrates ḥāʾ, idghām shamsī and madd. Once that block is solid, the other three stories sit on top of it.

    Does Tilmidh check madd length in Kahf?

    No. The mic confirms the skeleton of the word, not two or six ḥarakāt. On a long surah, that is enough to stop you on the missed word. Duration is a teacher’s ear.
